# ICP Construction Logic

## B2B ICP

Build the ICP around the **buying unit**, not just a job title.

### Required Fields
- **Company profile** (firmographics)
  - Industry / vertical (be specific — not "tech", say "B2B SaaS, 50–500 employees")
  - Revenue range or employee count
  - Stage (startup, growth, enterprise)
  - Geographic market
  - Tech stack or infrastructure signals (if relevant)

- **Champion** (person who finds and advocates for the solution)
  - Title / function
  - Day-to-day responsibilities
  - What does success look like for them personally?
  - Where do they learn and research? (publications, communities, events)

- **Decision Maker** (if different from Champion)
  - Title and authority level
  - What do they care about that the Champion doesn't?
  - Budget ownership?

- **Blockers** (who can kill the deal)
  - Typically: Legal, IT, Finance — note which are relevant and their concerns

- **Psychographic layer**
  - Core frustration the brand solves
  - What have they tried before that failed?
  - What does "good enough" look like (and why is the brand better)?
  - Buying trigger: what event causes them to start looking? (e.g., team growth, failed audit, new leadership)

### B2B Anti-ICP signals
- Company is too small to have the problem
- Buying process is too long / too committee-heavy for the brand's sales model
- Industry has structural reasons the solution won't stick (compliance, legacy systems)
- Champion lacks budget influence

## B2C ICP

Build around **identity + behavior**, not demographics alone.

### Required Fields
- **Demographics** (baseline, not the whole picture)
  - Age range
  - Income / spending capacity
  - Location type (urban, suburban, rural)
  - Life stage (student, early career, parent, retiree, etc.)

- **Psychographics** (this is where B2C positioning lives)
  - Values: what does this person believe in?
  - Identity markers: how do they see themselves? What tribes do they belong to?
  - Aspirations: who do they want to become?
  - Fears and avoidances: what are they running from?

- **Behavioral signals**
  - Where do they spend money in this category?
  - What triggers a purchase? (occasion, emotion, social proof)
  - What makes them loyal vs. what makes them churn?
  - Channels: where do they discover products? (social, search, word of mouth, retail)

- **Relationship with the category**
  - Are they category-aware and shopping actively, or do they need to be educated?
  - Are they brand-loyal elsewhere — and why?

### B2C Anti-ICP signals
- Price sensitivity exceeds what the brand's economics allow
- Values conflict with brand positioning (e.g., sustainability-brand targeting convenience-first buyer)
- Purchase frequency too low to sustain the brand's model

## Dual ICP (B2B2C or Multi-Sided)

If the brand sells to businesses who serve consumers (or has multiple customer types):
- Build a full ICP for each segment
- Note where their interests align and conflict
- Identify which segment the primary messaging should prioritize (usually the one who controls the purchase decision)
